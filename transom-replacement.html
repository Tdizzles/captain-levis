Why Transom Repair Matters

When a Transom Fails, the Problem
Goes Deeper Than the Surface.

The transom carries the engine’s weight and thrust, and ties into major hull structure. Water intrusion can rot the core, fiberglass can delaminate, and mounting areas can crack — putting your boat’s strength, safety, and performance at risk.

Common Transom Issues

  • Rotten or soft core
  • Cracks and delamination
  • Water intrusion and moisture damage
  • Engine mounting area failure
  • Loss of structural strength
  • Flexing under engine load

Complete Transom Replacement

Remove and replace the entire transom with marine-grade materials.

Once water has soaked the core across the transom and the wood has gone soft, a new core is the only lasting fix. We grind out the old core down to a clean, solid surface, fit a new marine plywood or composite core snug against the hull, bond it to the outer skin, and relaminate the inside face.

Partial Transom Repair

Repair localized damage while restoring strength and integrity.

If testing shows the damage is limited to one area, such as around a fitting or a mounting point, and the rest of the transom is solid, a partial repair can be the right call. We cut back to dry, sound material, rebuild the core and laminate in that area, and tie the new work into the existing structure.

Rotten / Water-Damaged Core

Replace wet, rotted, or delaminated core materials.

Water gets into a transom core through engine bolt holes, drain tubes and other fittings, and once it’s in, the wood rots and the transom starts to flex under engine load. We open the skin, remove all compromised core down to solid structure, and install new marine-grade core with the proper resin system before relaminating.

Get a Free Quote

Cracked or Delaminated Fiberglass

Repair structural cracks and separate laminates.

Cracks around the engine area or across the transom, or inner and outer layers pulling apart, usually mean the transom is flexing under engine load. We check whether the core behind is still sound, grind out the cracked and separated fiberglass, and rebuild it with marine-grade fabrics before fairing and finishing to match.

Get a Free Quote

Engine Mounting Area Repair

Rebuild damaged or crushed engine mounting areas.

Engine thrust and vibration can crush the core under the mounting bolts and stretch the holes out of round, which lets the engine shift and lets water in. We remove the damaged material, rebuild the mounting area solid with new core and laminate, and re-drill and seal the bolt holes so the engine sits tight.

Get a Free Quote

Transom Reinforcement

Additional reinforcement for high-horsepower or repowered boats.

A heavier or more powerful engine puts more load and thrust through the transom than the boat may have been built for. Before you repower, we inspect the transom and the structure it ties into, then add core and fiberglass reinforcement where it’s needed so the transom can handle the new engine without flexing.

Full Transom Replacement

What It Takes to Replace
a Transom Properly.

A sagging or rotten transom is not a patch-up job. Once water has reached the core and the wood has gone soft, the only lasting fix is a new core. The work is technical, but it comes down to respecting the boat’s structure one step at a time.

  1. 1

    Remove the rotten core

    The damaged core is cut and ground out until only a clean, solid surface is left. It is slow, gritty, hands-on work, and there is no shortcut: anything soft left behind will fail again.

  2. 2

    Cut and fit a new core

    The replacement core, marine plywood or a composite board, is measured, cut and fitted until it sits snug against the hull. The fit is what restores the transom’s strength.

  3. 3

    Bond and fasten

    The new core is bonded to the outer skin and securely fastened so the two work as a single piece.

  4. 4

    Relaminate the inside

    Layers of fiberglass and resin go on over the inside face, sealing the core in a tough, waterproof structural shell. This relamination is what rebuilds the strength and makes the repair last for years.

Frequently Asked Questions
How do I know if my transom is bad?

Common signs include soft spots, cracks, engine mounting movement, water leaks, or visible damage around the engine area.

Can you repair a partially damaged transom?

Yes. Many transoms can be partially repaired if the undamaged structure is solid.

Do you replace rotten transom cores?

Yes. We remove wet or rotted core material and replace it with new, marine-grade core materials.

Can you repair engine mounting damage?

Yes. We can rebuild damaged or crushed engine mounting areas and restore proper strength.

Do you offer mobile transom repair?

Yes. We offer both mobile service throughout Tampa Bay and shop repairs at our Largo location.

How long does transom repair take?

Turnaround time depends on the extent of the damage and access to the boat. We’ll provide a timeline after inspection.
