# How Captain Levi's quotes a repair (SEED — the owner will replace these rules with the shop's own)

Status: starter knowledge written from the shop's public pages and the Rev. 5 supplement format. Where a number or
rule below is not confirmed by Captain Dave or Levi, say so to the staff member instead of presenting it as policy.

## The estimate document
- One estimate per boat per job. Sections are lettered (A, B, C) by area of the boat or phase of the work;
  line numbers run continuously across sections. A good line has a short bold title on the first line and the
  detail on the lines below (what is done, where, with what). Example:
  "Grind back and re-laminate starboard transom corner\nRemove skin, cut out wet core 18 in × 14 in, dry, bond new
  marine plywood core, re-laminate 1708 biaxial with vinyl ester, fair."
- Labor is quoted in hours at the shop labor rate (default $125/hr; the document's pricing.laborRate wins).
  Materials are a lump sum per line in dollars. A materials-only line (gelcoat kit, hardware, haul-out fee that is
  a pass-through) has no labor.
- Sales tax: the shop's Rev. 5 document applies 7% (6% Florida + 1% Pinellas County) to the total repair charge.
  Keep that rule unless the staff member changes pricing.taxRate; flag that an accountant has not confirmed it.
- Notes go under the lines: what was observed, assumptions, exclusions, what happens if hidden damage is found.
- Terms: visible damage only, hidden damage reported with photos before extra work, estimate valid 30 days,
  work starts on approval, boat released on final payment. Do not promise completion dates in the document.

## The repair process the shop actually follows (every quote should map onto these steps)
1. Inspect and grind back to sound laminate.
2. Rebuild the structure: biaxial fabrics, woven roving, mat and core materials to a proper laminating schedule.
3. Bond with the right resin: epoxy or vinyl ester chosen for where the repair sits.
4. Vacuum bag or infuse when the job calls for it.
5. Fair the surface with epoxy fillers until it follows the original lines.
6. Spray the finish: gelcoat or marine polyurethane, color-matched.
7. Wet sand, long-board and buff (AQUABUFF compounds).

## Typical structure by job (use as a starting skeleton, then size the hours to the photos and description)
- Gelcoat scratch / chip / small gouge: 1 section. Lines: prep and grind; fill and fair; spray color-matched
  gelcoat; wet sand and buff. Materials: gelcoat kit, color match.
- Transom (full replacement): sections A Structural (cut skin, remove core, new core, re-laminate, re-tab),
  B Finish (fair, gelcoat/paint, buff), C Rigging (unbolt/re-hang motor, hardware) — rigging is often a separate line.
- Stringer / bulkhead: remove deck or access, cut out rotten section, new core, re-laminate and tab in, close up,
  finish. Note access work explicitly; it is where surprises live.
- Deck soft spot: open the skin, dry or replace wet core, re-laminate, fair, non-skid or gelcoat to match.
- Hull impact / collision: structural section first (laminate repair from inside when reachable), then finish.
- Detailing / oxidation: compound, polish, wax, by the foot of hull; interior and brightwork as separate lines.
- Boat yard: haul-out, blocking, pressure wash, storage per day/week, transport — materials-only / pass-through lines.

## What NOT to do
- Never invent a price for a part or a labor hour count you cannot justify from the description, photos, or the
  skeletons above. If the information is thin, put a placeholder line with 0 hours and tell the staff member
  what you need to size it.
- Never change pricing.laborRate, taxRate or taxNote on your own; ask first.
- Never promise a completion date, a guarantee, or an insurance payout in the document text.
- Keep the customer's and vessel's details exactly as given; do not "correct" a name or HIN.
