Understanding Hull Blisters

Blisters Start Small — What Matters Is How Deep They Go.

Osmotic blisters form when water passes through the gelcoat and reacts with the laminate, building pressure that lifts pockets of gelcoat or glass. A few shallow gelcoat blisters are a cosmetic issue; blisters in the laminate itself weaken the hull. We test and open a sample to find out which you have.

Blisters Shouldn’t Be Ignored

Blisters Are Water Getting In.

A handful of blisters can become hundreds. Left alone, osmosis keeps drawing water into the laminate and the repair grows with it.

  • Water AbsorptionWater in the laminate adds weight and keeps feeding blisters.
  • Laminate WeakeningDeep blisters break down the bond between glass layers.
  • DelaminationBlisters can grow into separated layers of fiberglass.
  • Bigger Repair BillsEarly repair and a barrier coat cost far less than a full peel.
Hydrolysis & Resin Choice

Why Blisters Form, and
What We Repair Them With.

Blistering on a hull bottom is hydrolysis: the laminate sits in constant contact with water with no epoxy barrier coat to keep it out. Water works through the gelcoat, reacts with the resin, and the pressure lifts the skin into blisters. Left unrepaired they grow in number and in size, and the bottom of the hull gets weaker.

Which resin a bottom is built from, and which one it is repaired with, decides how well it resists the next round. Three resins matter below the waterline.

  1. Most hulls

    General-purpose polyester

    What most fiberglass hulls are built from. It is the economical choice for production boatbuilding, and the one most prone to blistering when the bottom is left without a barrier coat.

  2. Below the waterline

    Vinyl ester

    Far more water-resistant, at roughly twice the price of polyester. A few builders lay up the whole hull in it; others use it only as a skin coat, the first layer of laminate behind the gelcoat. Fiberglass swimming pools follow the same pattern. It is what we use for blister repairs below the waterline.

  3. When the hull can’t dry out

    Epoxy

    About twice the price of vinyl ester again. We use it when a boat cannot stay out of the water long enough for the repair area to dry out fully, which a vinyl ester repair needs.

iWhichever resin the repair uses, the bottom gets an epoxy barrier coat afterwards.That is the layer the hull was missing in the first place, and it is what keeps the water out next time.

What Does Blister & Osmosis Repair Involve?

Blister repair starts with a moisture survey and opening sample blisters to see how deep they go. Blisters are ground open and washed, and the laminate is left to dry — for widespread osmosis, the gelcoat is peeled from the whole bottom. Damaged glass is replaced, the bottom is faired, and multiple coats of epoxy barrier coat seal it against future water.

  • Moisture survey and blister sampling
  • Grinding, washing and drying of blisters
  • Bottom peel for widespread osmosis
  • Laminate repair with epoxy and fiberglass
  • Epoxy barrier coat application
  • Fairing and bottom paint preparation

Cosmetic Blisters or Osmosis Damage?

Gelcoat Blisters

  • Small, scattered blisters
  • Only in the gelcoat layer
  • Laminate is dry and solid
  • Often on older or poorly cured gelcoat
  • Opened, filled and barrier coated

Laminate Osmosis

  • Large or widespread blisters
  • Blisters extend into the glass
  • High moisture readings across the bottom
  • Acidic, vinegar-smelling fluid
  • Needs peel, dry-out and re-lamination

Frequently Asked Questions

Got Questions?
We’ve Got Answers.

What causes blisters on a fiberglass hull?

Water slowly passes through the gelcoat and reacts with chemicals in the laminate, creating pressure pockets. Warm Florida water speeds the process up.

Are hull blisters dangerous?

Shallow gelcoat blisters are mostly cosmetic. Blisters in the laminate weaken the hull over time and should be repaired.

Do I need a full bottom peel?

Only for widespread or deep blistering. Many boats just need individual blisters opened, repaired and a barrier coat applied.

What is a barrier coat?

Several coats of epoxy applied below the waterline to slow water getting into the laminate. It’s the key to stopping blisters coming back.

Can blisters be repaired at my dock?

No — the boat has to be hauled and the laminate dried. We can haul and store your boat at our Largo yard.

How long does blister repair take?

Most of the time is drying. Small repairs take days; a full peel and dry-out can take weeks. We give you a timeline after the survey.
