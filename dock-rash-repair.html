Understanding Dock Rash

What is Dock Rash?

Dock rash happens when your boat rubs against docks, pilings, or trailers, causing scratches, scuffs or deeper gouges in the gelcoat and fiberglass. While it’s common, leaving it untreated can lead to oxidation, water intrusion, and more expensive repairs. We restore the damaged area so it blends seamlessly with the rest of your hull.

Get Your Dock Rash Repaired
Common Causes

How Dock Rash Happens

Dock Contact

Rubbing against docks, pilings, or seawalls can leave scratches, scuffs and gouges.

Trailer Bunks

Loading, unloading and long trailers can cause scuffs and gelcoat wear.

Tight Spaces

Maneuvering in marinas and narrow slips increases the risk of side impact damage.

Edges & Corners

High-contact areas like bow corners and rub rails are common spots for dock rash.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can dock rash be completely removed?

In most cases, yes. Light scuffs and surface scratches can often be wet sanded and polished out. Deeper gouges are ground back, filled and rebuilt with color-matched gelcoat, then blended so the repair disappears into the surrounding finish.

Will the repair be color matched?

Yes. We tint the gelcoat to your hull, including colors that have faded or oxidized over time, then blend the edges and polish so the repaired area matches the rest of the boat.

How long does a dock rash repair take?

Light scuffs can often be handled in a single visit. Deeper gouges take longer, because each layer of filler and gelcoat needs time to cure before it’s sanded and polished. We’ll give you a timeline with your quote.

Can you repair deep gouges from a dock?

Yes. We remove the damaged gelcoat, repair any exposed or damaged fiberglass underneath, then rebuild the surface with marine-grade materials and color-matched gelcoat. See our deep gouge repair page for more.
