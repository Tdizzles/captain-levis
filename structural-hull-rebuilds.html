Understanding Structural Rebuilds

Some Hulls Need More Than a Repair — They Need a Rebuild.

Years of water intrusion, a major impact or past repairs that failed can leave large areas of a hull and its internal structure compromised. A structural rebuild removes everything that’s damaged and rebuilds the hull skin, stringers, bulkheads and bottom as one bonded structure — often stronger than the original.

A hull that flexes, cracks or leaks at its structure is past cosmetic work. Rebuilding it properly is the only way to make it safe again.

What Does a Structural Hull Rebuild Involve?

A structural rebuild starts with a full survey of the hull and everything bonded to it. We strip out all wet, rotted and broken material, then rebuild the core, stringers, bulkheads and hull laminate with marine-grade materials and the right resin system for the job. Every new part is bonded and tabbed into the hull as one structure, then faired and finished.

  • Full structural survey and moisture testing
  • Removal of rotted core, stringers and bulkheads
  • Stringer and grid replacement
  • Bulkhead rebuilds and tabbing
  • Structural re-lamination of the hull skin
  • Fairing, gelcoat and finish work

Local Repair or Full Rebuild — What Does Your Hull Need?

Local Repair

  • Damage limited to one area
  • Surrounding laminate dry and solid
  • Stringers and bulkheads sound
  • No flex under load
  • Fixed with a targeted repair

Structural Rebuild

  • Damage across several areas
  • Wet or rotted core and stringers
  • Hull flexes or cracks under load
  • Failed past repairs
  • Needs sections of structure rebuilt

Frequently Asked Questions

Got Questions?
We’ve Got Answers.

Is a hull rebuild worth it?

For a boat with a good engine and a hull you want to keep, a structural rebuild usually costs far less than a comparable replacement boat. We’ll give you an honest assessment.

Will the hull be stronger than before?

Often, yes. Modern core and resin systems don’t rot, and we build to original spec or better.

Do you replace stringers and bulkheads too?

Yes. Stringers, bulkheads and core are part of most rebuilds and are bonded in as one structure.

Will the finish be color matched?

Yes. Repaired areas are faired, gelcoated to your hull color and polished.

Where is the work done?

Structural rebuilds are done at our Largo yard, where the boat can be stripped, dried and rebuilt properly.

How long does a structural rebuild take?

It depends on the scope — from a few weeks for a section to longer for a full grid and bottom. We give you a schedule with the quote.
